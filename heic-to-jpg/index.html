What HEIC is, and why your iPhone uses it

Since 2017, iPhones have saved photos in a format called HEIC instead of JPG. It stores the same picture in roughly half the space, which is why a phone full of photos lasts longer before it fills up. The catch is compatibility: many Windows PCs, older programs, web forms and some other phones still cannot open HEIC files at all.

How the conversion works here

A browser cannot read HEIC on its own, so when you open this tool — and only this tool — the page downloads a small, well-known reader library. That library decodes your photo on your device, and the tool saves it again as a standard JPG, the format every computer, phone and form accepts. The copy looks the same as the original to the eye; converting cannot add detail back, and because JPG stores pictures less efficiently than HEIC, the JPG can come out larger than the file you started with. That is normal.

When to convert, and when to keep the original

  • Convert when a form, website or shop will not accept the HEIC file.
  • Convert when you are sending photos to someone on Windows or Android.
  • Convert when a print shop or an office program needs a file it can open.
  • Keep the HEIC originals on your phone: they take less space, and things like Live Photos keep their motion only in the original.

Your photos stay on your device

iPhone photos are often the most personal files a person owns — family, children, documents photographed for safekeeping. Sending them to a conversion server means copies you cannot control. Here there is no server in the loop: the reader library is just code, it runs in your tab, and your photo is never sent anywhere.

Common problems and fixes

  • The conversion failed — a few HEIC variants, including some Live Photos, burst shots and depth photos, cannot be decoded in a browser at all. Open the photo in your Photos app and share or export it as JPG there, or convert a screenshot of it.
  • The JPG looks a little less vivid — some iPhone photos are captured in a wider range of colors than standard screens show, and the JPG is made for those standard screens. The difference is small, and the file opens everywhere, which is the point of converting.
  • It took a few seconds — big photos, especially from newer iPhones, hold a lot of pixels, and the decoding happens on your device rather than on a fast server. Give it a moment; nothing is stuck in an upload queue.

Questions

Is HEIC to JPG free here?

Yes, free with no account and no watermark.

Why can't my PC open HEIC photos?

HEIC is Apple's photo format. Many Windows programs and web forms do not support it yet; JPG works everywhere.

Are my photos uploaded?

No. The conversion runs in your browser. A small reader library is downloaded once so your browser can read HEIC — your photo itself never leaves your device.

Why did my HEIC file fail?

A few HEIC variants (some live photos and burst shots) cannot be read in a browser. Open the photo in the Photos app, share it as JPG, or take a screenshot and convert that instead.

Can I convert many HEIC photos at once here?

This tool takes one photo at a time, so each large file gets your device's full attention and nothing gets mixed up. If you mostly share with people who cannot open HEIC, your iPhone can also save future photos as JPG: in Settings, under Camera and Formats, choose Most Compatible — that changes new photos only, not your existing ones.

Does the JPG copy keep the photo's hidden details, like where it was taken?

No. The copy is drawn fresh in your browser, so the original's hidden details — including GPS location — do not carry over. That is usually a plus before sharing. If you need a copy with the details kept, share it from your Photos app instead.
